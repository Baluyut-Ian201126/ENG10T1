Scene 4
SCHOOL
Visual: Students cleaning and sorting waste on school grounds. Ian guides the group. Hans stands apart, looking unhelpful and lonely. Finally, everyone stands together with properly sorted bins.
Audio: Hopeful background music; gentle laughter.
Props: Three labeled bins, paper scraps, food waste, empty chemical cleaner bottle, gloves, brooms, trash bags, campaign poster.
Text on Screen:
“Learn to Sort—Protect Our World.”
Narrator: After witnessing how improper disposal caused real harm to their school grounds, Ian felt responsible to teach his friends the correct way to separate waste. He gathered everyone to practice proper waste segregation.
Ian:
(to friends, demonstrating clearly)
Let’s do this properly. Paper and cardboard go in the blue bin — for recyclables. Food scraps go in the green bin — for organic wastes. And anything hazardous, like leftover chemicals or cleaning solutions, always goes in the red bin. Proper waste segregation is the simplest way we can own up to our actions and practice proper waste segregation... Let's start!
(A friend accidentally places a crumpled sheet of paper into the organic bin. Everyone laughs gently.)
Abcde:
Oops! My mistake! I thought paper was organic because it comes from trees!
Ian:
(smiling, explaining patiently)
It’s a common mistake, but paper is processed material so it belongs in recyclables. Organic waste is only for things that naturally decay like food scraps or garden trimmings. Getting it right keeps our clean surroundings safe and protects our natural resources from contamination.
Narrator: While the group works, Ian notices Princess standing alone at the side, not helping—and secretly tossing an empty chemical bottle into the recyclables bin. The audience sees this clearly, but Ian does not yet know.
Ian:
(approaching kindly)
You haven’t joined us yet, Princess. Would you like to be part of our cleaning activity?
Princess:
I don’t really know where anything goes… and honestly, I’m afraid I’ll just mess everything up like everyone else did.
Ian:
That’s exactly why we’re here to learn together. Chemical safety means keeping dangerous items completely separate from other waste. Hazardous materials can poison soil and water if they mix with recyclable or organic waste. Everything has its proper place. Let me show you the rules step by step. Together, we can make a real difference for our school and our community.
Narrator: Princess nods, picks up gloves, and joins the group. They work side by side, helping one another sort every item correctly. The area becomes clean and orderly. Everyone steps back, feeling proud.
Ian:
(stands alone at center, speaking aloud with quiet conviction)
I used to sort things by color instead of by rule. I learned the hard way that guesswork cannot replace responsibility. Proper environmental protection begins with knowing where things truly belong — not where we prefer them to be. Every bin, every choice, every effort matters. We do not segregate simply because it is a school rule. We do it because we care about the air we breathe, the water we drink, and the land we share.
Narrator: He turns to face the audience, voice firm and passionate.
Ian:
So I say to every student, every teacher, every person watching: Sort your waste with care. Respect every resource that God has given us. Protect every living thing that shares this earth with us. Hazardous items belong in safe containers; recyclable materials deserve to be recovered and reused; organic matter can return to nourish the soil. When we place every kind of waste in its rightful place, we prevent pollution, safeguard health, and build a safer, cleaner, and better world for all!
Narrator: What seemed like a small task taught everyone a vital truth: waste segregation is more than just sorting — it is an act of love for our planet. Small changes, when done together, make a big difference for our world.

Text on Screen:
Don't waste the future—manage waste today.
